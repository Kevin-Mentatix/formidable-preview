Our approach

Legacy. Mastery. Freedom.

Three attributes of fulfilling work, in a deliberate order. It is how Nathaniel Koloc has thought about careers for more than a decade, and it is how Formidable runs a search.

Start a conversation →See services

What is the Legacy, Mastery, Freedom framework?

A model of fulfilling work developed by Nathaniel Koloc. Pursue legacy first (the impact you want), build the mastery that impact requires, then exchange mastery for freedom: pay, flexibility, and choice. In short, legacy guides mastery, which unlocks freedom.

1

Legacy

What will be better because of this work?

A higher purpose: the world is better after the work is done. Legacy comes first, because it decides which work is worth mastering at all.

Mastery

What craft will this person keep getting better at?

The craft of getting better at the skills the legacy requires. Mastery is how good intentions turn into real results.

Freedom

What choices does this role earn them?

Choosing who you work with, what you work on, and where and when. Freedom is what mastery is exchanged for as a career advances.

Why the order matters

Start with what you want to leave behind.

Most career advice, and most recruiting, starts at the wrong end. It leads with compensation and title (freedom) or with the skills on a résumé (mastery), and treats purpose as a nice-to-have.

The order matters because each step makes the next one possible. Knowing the impact you want tells you which skills are worth mastering. Real mastery is what earns real freedom. Skip the first step and you can get very good at work that does not matter to you.

Nathaniel's view is that the deepest career advantage comes from knowing what you want, not from piling up accomplishments. It is also why he has argued that "follow your passion" is incomplete advice: self-knowledge beats a résumé built by accident.

In a search

How the framework shapes the work.

  1. 01

    Scope the role around its legacy

    Before a single outreach, we define what the role must leave behind: the outcomes that will matter in five years, not just the bullet points that matter on day one.

  2. 02

    Search the people who are not looking

    The strongest candidates are usually busy doing great work. Reaching them takes relationships and a pitch that speaks to what they care about.

  3. 03

    Evaluate for mastery

    Track records are a start. We look for evidence of craft: how someone thinks, how they have grown, and how they make the people around them better.

  4. 04

    Close on freedom

    Great people choose where they work. We help you design an offer, from scope to compensation to flexibility, that a top candidate says yes to.

Two commitments

Hiring is the leader's job. Respect is everyone's.

You decide. Recruiting can be delegated. Hiring cannot. Formidable brings you a short list of people worth your time and helps you judge them, but the decision stays with the leader who will live with it.

Every candidate is treated well. Most people in a search will not get the job. Each of them should still come away respected and well informed, because that experience becomes your reputation.

Success means helping people. Nathaniel's simplest rule: professional success comes down to how much you help other people. A good search helps the organization, the person hired, and the people who were not.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Who created the Legacy, Mastery, Freedom framework?

Nathaniel Koloc, who leads Formidable. He has written about it in Harvard Business Review, including the 2013 piece "How to Build a Career Worth Having."

How does the framework help employers, not just job seekers?

It gives an employer a better pitch and a better filter. Roles described in terms of legacy, mastery, and freedom attract people who want the work itself, and those people tend to stay and grow.

Building something meant to outlast you?

Tell us about the role, the team, or the organization you are trying to build. Every conversation starts with what you want the work to leave behind.
