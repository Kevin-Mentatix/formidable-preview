A note from the builder

Why I built this.

This is an unofficial concept, not a Formidable website. I made it for Nathaniel ahead of our conversation about the Chief Growth Officer role. It is the clearest way I know to show how I think about story and distribution.

The thesis

Formidable's real pitch already exists. It is spread across nathanielkoloc.com, a decade of HBR and Palladium essays, and a client list most firms would lead with. The current homepage is six sector names and an email address. The work here was not inventing a story. It was assembling the one that is already true, and making it findable.

Five decisions, and why

  1. Lead with the worldview, not the services. Many firms can run a search. Very few can say why great people take a call they were not expecting. Legacy, Mastery, Freedom is that answer, so it gets the second-biggest section on the homepage and its own page.
  2. Keep the brand's DNA. The navy, cream, and gold, Young Serif headlines, and the real logo all come from formidable.services. I warmed it up with paper tones and photography of people and places, and set body text in Public Sans, the typeface the U.S. government uses for its own digital services. It is a quiet nod to civic work.
  3. Build one page per sector and per service. These are the searches a board member actually types, like "executive search climate" or "democracy reform executive director." The current site has nothing to rank for any of them. This version has 18 pages that each target one of those searches.
  4. Write for AI answers as well as Google. Every page opens with a plain-language "in short" answer, ends with an FAQ, and carries structured data (Organization, Person, Service, FAQ, Breadcrumb). There is an llms.txt so assistants like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude can describe Formidable accurately when someone asks who recruits for civic organizations.
  5. Make one conversion path, and make it human. No forms or funnels, just a clear "email us" and a short guide to what to say. For a high-trust, high-touch service, the inbox is the right front door.

What I would do in the first 90 days

Ship the site, then compound it: an essay archive built from Nathaniel's writing, a sector page that answers the top question each board asks, a quarterly "great work" brief for founders and family offices, and measurement tied to qualified conversations rather than traffic.
